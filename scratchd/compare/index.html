How Scratchd compares

Obsidian · Bear · Apple Notes · Drafts · iA Writer

Most of these are good apps, and for a lot of people they are the better choice. This page says which people, because a comparison that only lists wins is not worth reading.

The short version

Scratchd is for notes that are half prose and half config. If your notes are all prose, several apps below do that better. If you regularly paste a JSON payload or open a .proto schema next to your writing, that is the gap this fills.

Where Scratchd is the wrong choice

You want plugins. Obsidian's plugin ecosystem is the real product for a lot of its users, and there is nothing here that competes with it. Scratchd does a fixed set of things and you cannot extend it.

You think in links. There are no backlinks, no graph view and no transclusion. If your notes are a wiki, Obsidian is built for that and this is not.

You are not on a Mac. There is no Windows, Linux, Android or web version, and there are no plans for one. Obsidian runs everywhere.

You want to try before paying. The App Store has no trial for a one‑time purchase unless the app adds in‑app purchases, which Scratchd does not. Apple Notes and Obsidian are free to start.

You want a long track record. Bear and Obsidian have years of releases and thousands of users behind them. Scratchd is new and made by one person, and that is a real risk to weigh.

Where Scratchd is different

Structured formats get real viewers, not syntax highlighting. JSON and YAML render as browsable trees. A .proto schema gets a viewer where you can jump from a field type to its definition. Obsidian can approach some of this with plugins; nothing else here does it at all.

A diff view in the notes app. Put two files side by side, apply changes hunk by hunk, and save, on any two files you can reach rather than only notes in the library.

Files without the setup. Obsidian also keeps plain files in a folder you choose, so this is not a difference between the two. It is a difference from Bear, Apple Notes and Drafts, all of which keep your notes in a database only they can read.

Bought once. $4.99, no subscription, no in‑app purchases and no paid tier holding features back.

App by app

Obsidian

The closest thing to a direct comparison, and the one where Scratchd wins least. Both keep plain files in a folder you pick. Obsidian is free for personal use, runs on every platform, and has an enormous plugin ecosystem. Choose Scratchd over it only if you want the structured viewers and the diff without assembling them from plugins, and if Mac and iPhone are the only places you take notes.

Bear

Better looking, and better at long prose. It stores notes in its own database and exports on request, which is a different bargain from files on disk. It is a subscription.

Apple Notes

Free, already installed, and syncs without you thinking about it. If your notes are prose, lists and the occasional photo, it is genuinely hard to beat and this app is not trying to. It has no answer for structured formats and your notes live in its database.

Drafts

The best capture experience of the group, and the closest in spirit to the capture‑first idea. Its strength is what happens after capture: actions that route text elsewhere. Scratchd keeps the note instead of routing it, and stores it as a file.

iA Writer

A writing app rather than a notes app, and excellent at that. Plain files, strong typography, focused editing. If you are drafting rather than collecting, it is a better tool.

The honest summary

If your notes are prose, use Apple Notes or Bear. If they are a linked knowledge base, use Obsidian. If they are half prose and half config, and you are tired of your JSON looking like a wall of grey text in every app you have tried, that is the case for this one.
